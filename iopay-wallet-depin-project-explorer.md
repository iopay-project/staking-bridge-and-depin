## What a listing means

**It means the project exists and is indexed** by the explorer. That is all.

It does not mean the project is vetted, audited, profitable, endorsed by IoTeX, or recommended by this wallet. No page in this project has verified any project's economics, and no project pays for a place in a directory this page describes.

If that sounds like a low bar, it is: a listing is a starting point for your own checks, not a conclusion.

## What the explorer is useful for

- **Orientation.** Which DePIN projects are active in the IoTeX ecosystem, by category - wireless, sensors, storage, compute and so on.
- **Context for device income.** If you see earnings from a project in your dashboard, the explorer is where to learn what that project is.
- **Comparison of categories**, not of promised returns: two projects in different categories do different jobs, and comparing their reward rates without that context is meaningless.

## The five questions to ask before pointing hardware at anything

This is the part that actually protects you, and none of it requires trusting the wallet:

**1. What resource does it need?** Bandwidth, storage, sensor data, compute. If you cannot state it in one sentence, you do not yet understand the project.

**2. Who buys the service?** A project needs paying demand for the capacity it aggregates. If the only money in the system is the token reward, you are looking at a subsidy, and subsidies end.

**3. How are rewards funded?** Token emissions, fees from customers, or both. Emissions-funded rewards depend on the token's value; fee-funded rewards depend on real usage. They behave very differently when the market turns.

**4. What happens if the token falls 80%?** If your hardware becomes pointless at that point, the setup was a yield position with extra steps.

**5. Can the device be reused elsewhere?** Hardware you can repoint at another network is an asset; hardware locked to one project's economics is a bet.

Those five questions will filter harder than any star rating, and they are answerable from a project's own documentation rather than from a directory.

## "Which DePIN projects can I explore from the wallet?"

Q059, and the honest answer is structural: **the ones the explorer indexes.** That set changes as projects launch and wind down, which is why this page does not reproduce a list - a list copied here would be stale within a quarter and would look like a shortlist.

Open the explorer, read the categories, and treat each entry as a name to research rather than a choice to make.

## "Best DePIN projects"

There is no best in the abstract, and this page will not rank. The category is too varied - a wireless project and a storage project do not compete for the same hardware, the same buyer or the same risk - and the right project for a reader with a spare hard drive is the wrong one for a reader with rooftop space.

What you can do instead: pick the category that matches hardware you already own or can cheaply acquire, apply the five questions, and start small enough that being wrong is cheap.

## How the explorer connects to the rest of the wallet

- **Registration** makes a device visible: [ioID registration](https://github.com/iopay-project/staking-bridge-and-depin/blob/main/registering-an-ioid-device-with-iopay.md).

- **Burn-drop** is a chain mechanism tied to qualified staking buckets, not a project reward (F033).

## FAQ

**Which DePIN projects can I explore from the wallet?**
Whichever the explorer currently indexes. The set changes, so the explorer itself is the answer rather than a copied list.

**Does the explorer recommend projects?**
No. It lists them. A listing is not an endorsement, a vetting result or a forecast.

**Are the projects in the explorer safe?**
No claim of safety is made about any project, by the wallet or by this page. DePIN projects can fail, change their rewards or stop operating.

**Can I earn from a project without a device?**
Not from the device side. Some projects have token or staking mechanics, but that is a different activity with different risks - and not something a wallet directory can advise on.

**Where do I start if I already own hardware?**
Match the hardware to a category, apply the five questions, then register the device and track what it actually earns.

## What a directory cannot do

A directory cannot tell you what a project will pay next month, whether its hardware will still be wanted, or whether the operator will be there in a year. Those are the questions that decide whether a setup was worth building, and no list of names answers them.

What the explorer can do is bound the search: it tells you which categories exist, which names are active, and what each says it does. Everything after that is your own reading, and the five questions above are the shortest version of it that still works.
